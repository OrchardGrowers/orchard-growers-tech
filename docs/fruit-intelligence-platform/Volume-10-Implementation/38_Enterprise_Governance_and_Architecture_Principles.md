# Enterprise Governance and Architecture Principles

**Project:** Digital Fruit Intelligence Platform (DFIP)

**Platform:** eFruitMandi.live

**Company:** Orchard Growers Private Limited

**Document ID:** DFIP-V10-D38

**Version:** 1.0

**Status:** Enterprise Architecture

**Last Updated:** 2026-07-17

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines the Enterprise Governance Framework and the Architecture Principles that guide the evolution of the Digital Fruit Intelligence Platform.

These principles provide long-term consistency for business, technology, AI, hardware, security, and operational decision-making.

---

# 2. Objectives

The governance framework shall:

- align technology with business goals
- ensure architectural consistency
- reduce technical debt
- improve decision transparency
- protect long-term sustainability
- support innovation
- preserve platform integrity

---

# 3. Governance Layers

The platform governance consists of:

- Business Governance
- Product Governance
- Technology Governance
- Security Governance
- AI Governance
- Data Governance
- Operational Governance
- Compliance Governance

Each layer operates independently while remaining aligned with enterprise objectives.

---

# 4. Architectural Principles

The platform should remain:

- Modular
- Scalable
- Secure
- Observable
- Cloud-ready
- AI-ready
- Device-ready
- API-first
- Event-driven
- Backward compatible

These principles should guide all architectural decisions.

---

# 5. Technology Governance

Technology governance should define:

- approved technologies
- architecture reviews
- dependency management
- coding standards
- release standards
- upgrade policies
- lifecycle management

Technology choices should balance innovation with long-term maintainability.

---

# 6. Product Governance

Product governance should ensure:

- business alignment
- roadmap ownership
- feature prioritization
- stakeholder feedback
- usability validation
- release planning

Product decisions should remain driven by measurable business value.

---

# 7. Data Governance

Data governance should define:

- ownership
- classification
- quality standards
- retention policies
- privacy controls
- lifecycle management

Data should remain accurate, secure, and auditable.

---

# 8. AI Governance

AI governance should include:

- dataset ownership
- model lifecycle
- explainability
- validation
- monitoring
- ethical guidelines
- continuous improvement

AI systems should remain accountable and transparent.

---

# 9. Security Governance

Security governance should define:

- access policies
- identity management
- encryption standards
- vulnerability management
- incident response
- security audits

Security should be integrated into every stage of the platform lifecycle.

---

# 10. Decision Management

Major architectural decisions should include:

- rationale
- alternatives considered
- expected impact
- associated risks
- approval records
- review schedule

Decision history should remain permanently documented.

---

# 11. Continuous Improvement

Governance should support:

- architecture reviews
- operational reviews
- technology evaluations
- performance assessments
- security reviews
- innovation initiatives

Enterprise governance should evolve alongside business growth.

---

# 12. Design Principles

Enterprise Governance should remain:

- transparent
- measurable
- accountable
- scalable
- secure
- adaptable
- business-driven
- future-oriented
